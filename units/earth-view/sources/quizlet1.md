Earth's Equator,The line of equal distance around the Earth from the north and south poles. Cuts the Earth into northern and southern hemispheres. A 'real' line because the poles are real.
Meridian,A line stretching from the north to south pole, intersecting the equator at a right angle. Lines of longitude are meridians. All meridians meet at the north and south poles. They are arbitrary, any north-south line is a meridian.
Great Circle,Any circle around the Earth that has Earth's center as its center. Equivalent to the circumference of the Earth. Paths along great circles are the shortest distances between any two points on Earth. The equator is a great circle.
Parallel,Any line that runs east-west around the Earth. All lines of latitude are parallels. Parallels shrink in size as they run north and south of the Equator. Famous parallels are the tropics of Cancer and Capricorn and the arctic and antarctic circles.
The Prime Meridian,A line of longitude that goes through Greenwich, England, defining the Eastern and Western Hemispheres. Its position is arbitrary, and it was chosen for historical reasons.
The International Date Line,The line of longitude that cuts through the Pacific Ocean opposite the Prime Meridian. When you cross it, you move forward or back a whole day in standard time.

The Degree Confluence Project,An internet project that maps and catalogs as many points as possible where Latitude and Longitude lines meet as whole numbers
Eastern Standard Time,Pomfret's Time Zone, one of 4 in mainland US
Equatorial Coordinate System,The projection of the Latitude and Longitude system into the sky to define celestial coordinates
Right Ascension (RA),The equivalent of Longitude in the sky, the east-west coordinate in the Equatorial system. Measured in Hours, Minutes, and Seconds.
Declination (DEC),The equivalent of Latitude in the sky, the north-south coordinate in degrees above or below the Celestial Equator. Measured in Degrees, Minutes, and Seconds.
Celestial Equator,A projection of the equator of Earth into the sky. It is tipped approx. 23.5 degrees to the ecliptic plane that the Earth orbits the sun on.
Celestial Poles,The projection of the poles of the Earth into the sky, the points right above the north and south poles.
Azimuthal Coordinate System,A coordinate system based on the observer instead of the lines on Earth. Altitude and Azimuth are measured in Degrees, Minutes, and Seconds.
Zenith,The point directly over the observer's head
Nadir,The point in the sky directly below an observer's feet, straight through the Earth, above the antipodes
Antipodes,The point on the Earth directly opposite the observer, has the opposite sign on the Latitude and Longitude
Altitude,In the azimuth coordinate system, the number of degrees above the horizon of an object in the sky. Measured in Degrees, Minutes, Seconds
Azimuth,In the azimuth coordinate system, the number of degrees east to west of an object in the sky, starting north and moving east in a full circle. Measured in degrees, minutes, and seconds.
Tropic of Cancer,A line of latitude at about 23.5 degrees north of the equator. It represents the most northern limit of the sun being at the zenith at the summer solstice.
Tropic of Capricorn,A line of latitude at about 23.5 degrees south of the equator. It represents the most northern limit of the sun being at the zenith at the summer solstice.
Arctic Circle,A line of latitude at 66.5 degrees north. North of this line, the sun can be up for 24 hours in a day (summer) or never rise (winter). The further north you go, the longer these periods can be.
Antarctic Circle,A line of latitude at 66.5 degrees south. South of this line, the sun can be up for 24 hours in a day (our winter) or never rise (our summer). The further south you go, the longer these periods can be.
Time for a full rotation of Earth,1 day; 23 hours and 56 minutes; roughly 86,400 seconds
Time for a full revolution of the Earth,1 year, 365.26 days
Leap year,The adjustment we make for a year being 1/4 day longer than 365 days -- we add one day every four years.
Winter Solstice,Roughly December 21, the day the sun 'stops' moving lower in the sky. The point of maximum tilt away from the sun in the northern hemisphere. The 'shortest day of the year.' The sun is directly overhead on the Tropic of Capricorn.
Summer Solstice,Roughly June 21, the day the sun 'stops' moving higher in the sky. The point of maximum tilt toward from the sun in the northern hemisphere. The 'longest day of the year.' The sun is directly overhead on the Tropic of Cancer.
Autumnal Equinox,'Fall' equinox, roughly September 21, a day when the sun and Earth's tilt are perpendicular, causing equal day and night. The sun is directly overhead at the equator.
Vernal Equinox,'Spring' equinox, roughly March 21, a day when the sun and Earth's tilt are perpendicular, causing equal day and night. The sun is directly overhead at the equator.
Celestial Sphere,An imaginary sphere around the Earth that the stars are fixed onto. It is the analogue to the globe, the context for the equatorial coordinate system.
Celestial Equator,The projection of Earth's equator into the sky, the line of zero declination, dividing the sky into northern and southern hemispheres.
Celestial Poles,The projection of the Earth's north and south poles into the sky, the points which all the other stars seem to move around in a circle. They define the axis of rotation for the celestial sphere. 90 degrees declination.
Ecliptic,The line in the sky that represents the path of the sun. The moon and planets also follow it closely. It is in reality the perception of the flat disc of the solar system. It is angled to the Celestial Equator by 23.5 degrees, matching the Earth's tilt. When the moon and sun line up on the Ecliptic, an Eclipse occurs (lunar or solar, depending)
Perihelion,Around January 3, the point in Earth's orbit closest to the Sun.
Aphelion,Around July 3, the point in Earth's orbit furthest from the Sun.
The Tropics,The zone between the tropics of Cancer and Capricorn, including the Equator. This is the zone where the sun can be directly overhead at some point in the year.
Analemma,A figure drawn out by the sun's position if tracked throughout a year. On Earth, it looks like a skinny figure 8. The top and bottom are the solstices, and the crossing point shows the equinoxes.
Seasons,Changing temperatures on the Earth due to the tilt of the Earth and sunlight. Winters are cold because the sun is not in the sky as long AND the sunlight comes in at a low angle. Summers are hot because the sun is up in the sky a longer time and the rays come in directly.
Counter-Clockwise,The direction of the Earth's orbit AND the direction of Earth's rotation when looking down on the north pole
Earth's Tilt,About 23.5 degrees, stays consistent over hundreds of years. The primary cause of the seasons and Earth's habitability.
Terminator,The border between daylight and night on the Earth or any body in space. The 'twilight zone.' Sunrise or sunset are occurring on this line.
Aurora borealis,The northern lights, caused by charged particles spiraling along magnetic fields creating a glow. Occurs in the north and south (Aurora Australis in south)
